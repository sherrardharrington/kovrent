FAQ

Straight answers, before the first call.

Kovrent is a permanent holding company that buys SEC-registered RIAs from founders and holds them: no fund term, no resale, no rebrand. The answers below cover price, process, confidentiality, and what changes for your team and clients.

What size firms does Kovrent acquire?

SEC-registered RIAs, $100M to $1B in AUM.

Do I have to leave when I sell?

No. Some founders stay for years, some transition in twelve months. The timeline is part of the deal and you help set it.

Will my team keep their jobs?

Keeping the team is core to our model. We buy firms because of the people serving the clients, and our deals are structured around continuity, not cuts.

Does my firm's name change?

No. Your name stays on the door by default. Changing it is your call, not ours.

Do you only buy off-market?

We prefer it. Off-market means a quieter process, no auction pressure, and more time to get the fit right. If you're already working with a banker, we'll still take the call.

How much is my RIA worth?

It depends on recurring revenue, growth, margins, and how much of the firm runs without you. Anyone quoting a multiple before seeing your numbers is guessing. We'll show you a real number with the math behind it.

I'm an advisor at a firm Kovrent is acquiring. What happens to me?

Your clients stay with you, your role continues, and you gain a growth path that doesn't end when the founder retires.

Who funds Kovrent, and can your investors force a sale of my firm?

Kovrent acquires with partner capital and seller financing. Every partner has their own capital in every deal. As we add investors, they sign up for the same terms our partners did: own advisory firms for the long term, with no date by which anything has to be sold. Structurally: no fund term, no redemption right, and no provision anywhere in our documents that lets anyone force the sale of an operating firm. Ask us in the first meeting and we'll walk you through the capital structure line by line. If a buyer won't show you the plumbing, that's your answer about the plumbing.

How long does a deal take, start to finish?

Sixty to ninety days from signed LOI to close in most cases. The first conversation to an LOI depends entirely on you, and some founders take two years over it.

What does diligence actually cover?

Financials, client and revenue concentration, the advisory agreements, compliance history and ADV filings, employment and comp arrangements, vendor contracts, and technology. You see the full request list before anything is sent, and it does not grow midstream.

Will my clients be told before closing?

Only by you, and only when you decide. Nothing is announced publicly until you have told the people you want to tell in the order you want to tell them.

How confidential is this really?

Nothing leaves the four partners without your written consent. We do not run your name past lenders, we do not maintain a marketed pipeline, and we do not announce closed deals until the founder approves the words.

What multiple do you pay?

It depends on recurring revenue, client retention, growth, margin, and how much of the firm runs without you. Anyone quoting a multiple before seeing your numbers is guessing at one. Market EBITDA multiples for platform buyers were reported in the 9-16x range at the end of 2025, which tells you the spread is wide and the details decide where you land. Source: DeVoe Annual RIA M&A Outlook, December 2025.

Do you use earnouts?

Rarely, and we would rather not. An earnout asks you to hit targets using levers you no longer fully control. When one is genuinely necessary, the targets are things you actually influence, and they are written in one page of plain English.

How much of the deal is cash at close?

The majority, with Kovrent equity on top. Both land the day we sign. The exact split is set in the LOI and we show you the math behind it.

What is Kovrent equity, in plain terms?

Ownership in the holding company for part of the price. You stop being an RIA owner and become a partner in the company that owns your firm and every firm we buy after it. It carries information rights, so you see the same financials we do, and it keeps compounding if the whole thing grows.

Am I signing a non-compete?

Yes, and you should expect one from any buyer. Scope, length, and geography are negotiated, and we will explain what each clause is actually protecting rather than handing you boilerplate.

Do I need an investment banker to talk to you?

No. Most of our conversations start without one. Have your attorney and your CPA read anything you sign, always.

Will my technology stack change?

Some of it, over the first year: the back-office layer, reporting, and compliance workflow. Client-facing tools change only when your advisors agree the replacement is better, and the schedule is set with your operations lead.

What is the AI infrastructure I keep hearing about, and is it safe?

It automates internal work: meeting notes, data entry, filing preparation, and reporting. It does not make investment decisions and it does not talk to your clients. It runs inside the same SEC-grade compliance framework as everything else, which means access controls, audit logs, and books-and-records retention. Ask a partner for a walkthrough.

What if I want to keep working for another ten years?

Then keep working. The timeline is part of the deal and you help set it. What changes is that the firm no longer depends on you being there.

What happens if you and I disagree after closing?

Governance is written down before you sign: who decides what, and where your consent is required. Read those clauses more carefully than the price.

What makes you walk away?

Revenue that lives entirely in one person, compliance history we cannot get comfortable with, a team the founder will not let us talk to, or a process where the highest number this quarter is the only criterion.

What should I ask any buyer that I have not asked you?

Whose money is it, how long can it hold, what forces a sale, and what will I be allowed to see afterward. Ask us the same four and hold us to the answers.
