Three Models

Private equity, consolidator, or permanent capital.

A private equity fund typically resells your firm within five to seven years. A consolidator typically retires your brand. A permanent holding company buys to hold, which changes what happens to your name, your team, and your clients after closing.

Last updated: August 2026

All three buy RIAs. All three are the right answer for somebody. The differences that matter are the hold period, who owns the client relationship afterward, and what forces a second sale. Below is the honest version, including where we are the wrong buyer.

Definitions

Private equity fund

A pool of outside capital raised with a stated life. The manager is obligated to return that capital, so every business it buys is bought with an exit in mind. That is not a criticism, it is the job. It means the question is never whether your firm gets sold again, only when and to whom.

Consolidator

A national advisory firm that acquires practices and merges them into one operating company: usually one brand, one service model, one investment platform. Done well, it gives clients depth a small firm cannot afford on its own. What you trade is the thing that made your firm distinctive.

Permanent holding company

A company that buys firms to own them, funded by capital with no return-by date. It grows slower, pays less at the top of a cycle, and depends entirely on whose money sits behind it. No fund behind us means no fund clock. Ask, and a partner will walk you through the capital structure line by line.

Common Questions

What actually makes capital permanent?

There is no fund with a stated life behind it, so there is no date by which anything has to be sold. No fund behind us means no fund clock. Ask, and a partner will walk you through the capital structure line by line.

Is a private equity buyer a bad outcome for my firm?

No. A buyer underwriting an exit can often pay the highest number today, and if price is what you are optimizing for, that is a rational choice. The tradeoff is that a second owner is part of the plan and you will not be the one choosing them.

What is the real difference between a consolidator and a holding company?

A consolidator merges the firms it buys into one operating company, usually one brand and one service model. A holding company owns them as separate firms. Consolidation buys clients depth a small firm cannot afford; separation keeps what made your firm worth buying.

Could Kovrent still sell my firm someday?

Nothing in our structure requires it: no fund term, no redemption right, no provision that puts your firm back on the market. Any sale would be a decision rather than an obligation, and your Kovrent equity comes with information rights, so you would see the same numbers we do.

How much of the market is backed by private equity?

Enough that most founders will meet it. When are you the wrong buyer?

When you want the top of the market, when you want to be gone in ninety days, or when four bidders and a banker are already running a process. We would rather be early than highest, and we will tell you that on the first call rather than the fifth.

How do I check any of this?

Ask the same four questions of every buyer: whose money is it, how long can it hold, what forces a sale, and what will I be allowed to see afterward. A buyer who cannot answer those in plain sentences has told you something.
